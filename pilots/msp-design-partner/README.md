# MSP Design-Partner Pilot Kit

The point of this pilot is to answer one question as cheaply as possible: **will MSPs rely on the evidence pack and pull it toward more of their clients?** You are not selling software. You are hand-delivering an outcome and watching what they do. Their behavior — not their compliments — is the signal.

Target: **3–5 design-partner MSPs**, roughly 5–40 techs each (owner still makes tool decisions, feels labor pain personally). Timebox: **4 weeks**.

---

## Part 1 — Recruiting the design partners

### Where to find them

- **r/msp** — read it daily before you post anything. Search it for people complaining about compliance/insurance questionnaires eating their techs' time. Those complaints are your inbound list.
- MSP Slack/Discord communities and peer groups.
- Warm intros first if you have any — an MSP owner who already knows you is worth ten cold ones.

**Rule:** show up as a helpful analyst, not a vendor. Answer hard questions in public with real substance and no pitch for a week or two before you ask anyone for anything. The DMs start coming to you.

### The opening message

Send this as a DM or email after you've had *some* genuine interaction, or as a warm-intro follow-up. Keep it short. The honesty is the strategy — it removes the skepticism a normal vendor pitch triggers.

> Hey [name] — I'm a cyber analyst, not a salesperson, and I'm building something specifically for MSPs dealing with the compliance/cyber-insurance questionnaire mess their clients keep dumping on them.
>
> I'm not trying to sell you anything. I'm looking for a few MSPs to build it *with* — you'd get it free, and in exchange I'd want your honest feedback and a real client or two to test it against.
>
> Right now, when one of your clients gets a cyber-insurance renewal questionnaire or an audit request, how are you handling it? Is that landing on you as unbillable tech time?
>
> If that's a real headache, I'd love 20 minutes to hear how you deal with it today. Worst case you get a free tool out of it.

### Why this works

- "Not a salesperson / build it with you / free" disarms the reflexive no.
- It leads with *their* pain in *their* words (unbillable tech time, questionnaires dumped on them), not your product.
- It asks a diagnostic question, not for a sale.
- The ask is tiny: 20 minutes.

Send to ~15–20 MSPs to land 3–5. Expect most to ignore you. That's normal, not a signal about the idea.

## Part 2 — The pilot runbook

### The diagnostic call (20 min, before you build them anything)

Goal: confirm the pain is real *for this MSP* and find the trigger. You're diagnosing, not pitching. Talk 20% of the time.

Ask, in this order:

1. **Why now / what triggers it?** When does a client's compliance or insurance need actually land on you? (Renewal? Audit? A client lost a deal over a security question?) → tests for the compelling event.
2. **Walk me through what you do today.** Spreadsheet? A tech manually answering? Nothing? → the current substitute reveals willingness to pay.
3. **What does that cost you?** How many tech-hours per client, and is any of it billable? → sizes the labor pain.
4. **What happens if you get it wrong?** → stakes.
5. **Who else touches this?** → the buyer/decision map.

Then shut up and listen. Write down their exact phrases — they become your marketing copy. You've done well if you leave knowing their **trigger**, their **current substitute**, and whether there's a **deadline**.

### Deliver the pack manually

- Pick one real client of theirs (anonymized as needed).
- Use your manual-pilot tooling to produce a clean, **their-branded** evidence pack for that client.
- Hand it over. Watch the reaction. Do they immediately see how they'd put it in front of the client?

### Then wait, and watch behavior

Deliver, then deliberately don't chase. Do they come back? The whole test is in what they do *without you pushing*.

### The cadence

- **Week 1:** land calls, run diagnostics.
- **Week 2:** deliver first packs.
- **Week 3:** deliver a second round / a second client. See if they *ask* for more.
- **Week 4:** read the signals, decide.

## Part 3 — Reading the signals: build vs. walk

### Green — build it

- The MSP **relies** on the pack: asks for it again, gets antsy when it's late.
- They **use it in front of their own client** — it entered their actual workflow.
- **Strongest signal:** they ask "can I get this for my other clients too?" or offer to pay. An MSP pulling it toward more clients *is* the channel validating itself.
- Multiple MSPs want roughly the **same core** thing.

### Red — walk away (or rethink)

- Polite enthusiasm, then no usage. "Love it, keep me posted" is a **no**.
- They took the free pack and never engaged again.
- Every MSP wants something completely **different** — no common core means you have a consulting business, not a product.
- Producing each pack takes enormous manual labor with **no plausible path to automating** it — the real product may not scale even though the need is real.

### The honest bar

If MSPs won't rely on the *free, manual* version, they will definitely not pay for the *software* version. Learning that now costs you a few weeks of spreadsheets instead of a year of engineering. A clean "walk away" is a win — it's the pilot doing its job.

## Part 4 — Follow-up sequence

Use sparingly. The goal is to observe pull, not manufacture it. One nudge, then let their behavior speak.

**After the diagnostic call (same day):**

> Thanks for the time, [name]. Based on what you described — [play back their exact pain in their words] — I'll put together an evidence pack for [client] this week so you can see the actual output. No commitment; I just want your honest read.

**When you deliver the pack:**

> Here's the pack for [client], branded as yours. This is the thing you'd hand the client. Two questions: (1) Does this look like something you'd actually put in front of them? (2) What's wrong or missing? Be brutal — that's the whole point of you being a design partner.

**One week after delivery, if silent (single nudge only):**

> Quick one — did the [client] pack end up being useful, or did it miss the mark? Genuinely fine either way; a "not useful" tells me as much as a yes.

**If they ask for more clients (the green signal — respond fast):**

> Absolutely. Send me the details for the others and I'll turn them around. And honestly — this is exactly the signal I was hoping for. Let's talk about what an ongoing version looks like for your whole book.

---

## The one thing to remember

You are buying information, not selling a product. Every pack you hand over is a probe. The MSPs' unprompted behavior over these four weeks tells you — more honestly than any projection — whether you're on the road. Run it, then let the signal decide the next move.
